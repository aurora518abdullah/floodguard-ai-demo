01 — THE HUMAN PROBLEM

A flood is a water event.
Its impact is an information challenge too.

When conditions change, people need to understand what is happening near them, what it could mean, and what sensible steps they can take.

THE REALITY

One situation. Many questions. Information in different places.

RAINFALL · RIVER CONDITIONS · LOCAL PREPAREDNESS
THE INFORMATION GAP

People don't just need more data. They need data they can understand.

Weather updates, river information and safety guidance may come from different sources. For a household trying to prepare, connecting those pieces can be difficult — especially when time and connectivity are limited.

And a general update may not answer a very local question: “What should my family pay attention to right now?”

The opportunity: make relevant information clearer, more local and more actionable — without replacing official warnings.
CHALLENGE 01

Fragmented information

People may have to move between multiple sources to build a picture of local conditions.

CHALLENGE 02

Local context

Flood conditions vary across regions. A broad update may not communicate local context clearly.

CHALLENGE 03

Actionable guidance

Understanding conditions is only one part; families also need practical preparedness information.

02 — THE IDEA

Meet FloodGuard AI.

A single experience designed to connect information with preparedness — starting with Bangladesh.

OUR APPROACH

From scattered signals to a clearer picture.

FloodGuard AI is a prototype concept that brings together weather context, experimental machine-learning risk estimation and preparedness guidance.

It is not designed to tell people to ignore official alerts. It aims to help users understand information and think about preparation.

      05 — THE AI BEHIND THE IDEA

      Not AI for the label.
      AI for a specific question.

      Can historical station observations help estimate whether a flood event may occur within the next 24 hours? That is the classification problem explored in our ML experiment.

      WHAT THE METRICS MEAN

      Evaluation, not a guarantee.

      ROC-AUC measures ranking discrimination. PR-AUC helps assess performance when positive flood labels are relatively uncommon. These validation results do not prove operational accuracy in every region or season.

      CURRENT TECHNICAL CONTEXT

      Built as an experiment.

      Python, tabular ML, FastAPI for the local prediction API, a browser-based frontend and Open-Meteo weather context in the prototype.

      Be transparent: the model is experimental. The next step is stronger regional validation, calibrated estimates, reliable data integration and expert review.

      08 — BEFORE YOU ASK

      Questions we expect.
      Honest answers.

      Tap a question to open the answer.

      How accurate is the model?

      Validation ROC-AUC is 0.8732 and PR-AUC is 0.3253. Both are experimental results. Floods are rare in the data, which is why PR-AUC is lower. Calibration and expert review come next.

      Does this replace official warnings?

      No. FloodGuard AI is a student prototype. It tries to make information easier to understand and does not issue authoritative alerts.

      What works today?

      The live prototype has weather context and the first ML experiment. Recovery support and wider guidance are still concepts, and we label them that way.

      Why a 24-hour window?

      It turns a vague worry into one question that can be tested: will a flood event occur within the next 24 hours?

      What would you do with more time?

      Test across regions and seasons, calibrate the estimates, bring in reliable official data and ask communities what they actually need.
